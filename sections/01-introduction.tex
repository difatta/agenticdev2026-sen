\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}

In a few years, the use of large language models (LLMs) in software engineering has moved from code completion to agents that plan, invoke tools, run tests and edit whole repositories~\cite{hou2024llm4se}. Repository-level benchmarks such as SWE-bench~\cite{jimenez2024swebench} turned the resolution of real issues into a measurable task, and agent--computer interfaces~\cite{yang2024sweagent} and open platforms~\cite{wang2025openhands} showed that agents can navigate a codebase and validate their own patches. At the same time, a fixed, three-phase pipeline proved competitive with far more autonomous agents~\cite{xia2025agentless}, raising the question of how much autonomy, and how much structure, agents actually need. Role-based multi-agent systems promise further gains through specialisation~\cite{he2025mas4se}, but recent analyses trace their failures to problems of specification, inter-agent coordination and verification~\cite{cemri2025mast} and show that, without central verification, errors propagate between agents~\cite{kim2025scaling}.

These developments raise questions that benchmark accuracy alone does not answer: how agents should be grounded in a project's knowledge, when their loops are reliable, what they really cost, how their outputs can be trusted, and how they reach beyond code into requirements, architecture, testing and assurance. AgenticDev was created as a forum for these questions. Its scope covered agent architectures and coordination mechanisms, human--AI collaboration and the integration of agents into engineering workflows, with trustworthiness, human oversight, verification and validation, explainability and empirical evaluation as central concerns. The workshop drew inspiration from Horizon Europe projects investigating complementary approaches to AI-supported software engineering and collaborative agent communities: AI4SWEng~\cite{proj:ai4sweng}, ASTIR~\cite{proj:astir}, MOSAICO~\cite{proj:mosaico}, HIVEMIND~\cite{proj:hivemind}, and FAITH~\cite{proj:faith}.
