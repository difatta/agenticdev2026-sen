This report summarises the First International Workshop on Agentic AI for Next-Generation Software Development (AgenticDev~2026), held on 12~October~2026 in Munich, Germany, co-located with the IEEE/ACM International Conference on Automated Software Engineering (ASE~2026). The workshop brought together researchers and practitioners studying how agents built on large language models, which plan, use tools and collaborate, can support software engineering across the lifecycle. It received 84 submissions and accepted 26 papers (11 long, 8 short and 7 extended abstracts), and opened with a panel of Horizon Europe projects on agentic software engineering. We group the contributions into five research directions: grounding agents in the repository; reliability of agentic loops; evaluation methodology; engineering multi-agent systems; and agents across the lifecycle. From the papers and the panel we distil five lessons: the harness is part of the system; structure makes small and local models viable; agent outputs need provenance and bounded authority; evaluation must measure cost and correctness on realistic, lifecycle-spanning tasks; and multi-agent collaboration must be shown to pay for its coordination costs. We close with the open challenges these lessons pose to the community.
